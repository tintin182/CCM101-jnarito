# 💭 Mission Reflection

This mission gave me a better understanding of how cloud platforms work and how different companies may have different reasons for choosing one platform over another. The cloud platform that impressed me the most was **AWS**. I found its range of services interesting because it can support many different types of workloads, from small startups to large global applications. I also liked how resources can be increased or reduced depending on demand.

One thing I noticed when comparing AWS, Azure, and GCP is that they have many similarities. All three provide services for computing, storage, networking, databases, and other common cloud needs. The names may be different, but many services have similar purposes. For example, **Amazon EC2, Azure Virtual Machines, and Google Compute Engine** can all be used to run virtual servers.

I also learned that business requirements have a big influence on cloud platform selection. There is no single platform that is automatically the best for every company. A startup with a limited budget may have different needs from a university that already uses Microsoft technologies. An AI research company may also prioritize computing power and machine learning services. Factors such as budget, existing technology, workload, scalability, and future plans all need to be considered.

Thinking like a **Cloud Architect** helped me look beyond simply learning what each cloud service does. I had to consider which platform and services would actually make sense for a particular situation. This made me think more about the reasons behind a cloud architecture rather than just the technology itself.

Finally, I think my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio has improved because it now shows the work I completed throughout the mission, including cloud research, comparisons, recommendations, and Linux investigation. It gives a better picture of what I learned and how I applied that knowledge to different situations.
